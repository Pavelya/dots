Pinfold privacy policy

Last updated: 25 November 2026

Pinfold is a game of dots for Android phones, made by School 25, the developer named on Pinfold's Google Play page. This page says what the game keeps on your phone, what it sends, to whom, and why. If anything here is unclear, write to pinfoldsupport@gmail.com.

The short version

  • There is no account and no sign-in. The game never asks for your name, email address, contacts, photos or location.
  • Your progress, settings and saved games stay on your phone.
  • The game sends Google crash reports and a few game events, so we can fix bugs and see where the game is too hard. They carry a random number for the installation, never your name.
  • Ads come from Google AdMob, and only when you choose to watch one for a booster.
  • The game does not use your phone's advertising ID.
  • We do not sell your data, and we give it to no one but Google, as described below.

What stays on your phone

Your campaign progress and stars, your boosters, the daily puzzles you solved, the totals on Stats, your settings, unfinished games, the Shop items Google Play says you own, how many ads you watched today, and your answer to Google's consent form. The game does not send any of this anywhere.

One exception: if Android's backup is on for your phone, Android copies your campaign progress (nothing else) to your Google account, so a new phone can bring it back. Google does this under its own terms, and we cannot see the backup.

What leaves your phone

Everything below goes to Google, through Google's own libraries inside the game. Each is encrypted on the way.

Crash reports: Firebase Crashlytics

What
When the game crashes: which part of the game failed (the stack trace), the game's state at that moment, facts about the phone such as its model, Android version and free memory, and a random ID for this installation of the game (the Crashlytics installation ID and the Firebase installation ID).
Why
To find and fix crashes.
When
In every country, from the first time the game starts, because a crash can't be fixed without its report.
Who gets it
Google, which processes it for us.
Kept for
90 days.

Game events: Google Analytics for Firebase

What
That the game was opened for the first time; how often and how long it is played; when a campaign level starts and ends, with the level, whether it was passed and the stars; when a booster is used, and which; when a daily puzzle ends, and whether it was solved; and purchases in the Shop (the item, its price and currency), which Google Play reports to Analytics by itself. With them go a random ID for this installation of the game (the app instance ID), the phone's model and Android version, and the country and region, which Google works out from the phone's internet address.
Not sent
No advertising ID, no Android ID, no name and no account. Google signals are off, and the data is not shared with Google's advertising products.
Why
To see where players get stuck or stop playing, and make those levels better.
When
In the EU, the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, nothing is sent until you have answered Google's consent form. If you decline, the events are still sent, but Analytics keeps no ID on your phone, so they can't be linked to each other or to you and only add to totals. Elsewhere, from the first time the game starts.
Who gets it
Google, which processes it for us.
Kept for
2 months. After that, only totals remain, such as how many players passed level 3.

Ads: Google AdMob and Google's consent form

What
When the game asks Google for an ad and while one plays: the phone's internet address (from which Google estimates a rough location), the app set ID (a random ID Android gives the apps of one developer on a phone; it is not the advertising ID), how you interact with the ad (for example taps and how much of the video played), and technical facts such as how long the ad took to load.
Why
So Google can choose an ad and show it, count it so that the game is paid for it, and stop fraud. Every ad is limited to content rated PG.
Who gets it
Google, which uses it as described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and in Google's privacy policy.
Your choices
In the EU, the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form opens the first time the game starts. It asks what Google may do with your data for ads, for example whether ads may be personalised. You can change your answer at any time in Settings > Privacy > Ad choices. In every country, Settings > Privacy > Show ad offers turns off every offer to watch an ad.
Kept for
As long as Google's retention rules allow.

Purchases: Google Play Billing

Shop items are bought through Google Play. Google Play takes the payment, under Google's privacy policy; the game never sees your card or other payment details. Google Play tells the game only which items you own, and the game keeps that on the phone. Google Analytics also counts each purchase, as said above.

Reviews: Google Play In-App Review

Now and then, after a level won with three stars, the game asks Google Play to show its review card. If you rate or review the game there, the review goes to Google Play, which publishes it like any other review on Google Play.

When you write to us

Settings > Send feedback opens your email app with our address, the game's version, the Android version and the phone model filled in; you can change or delete any of it before sending. We use your email only to answer you, keep it only as long as we need it for that, and never add you to a mailing list.

What the game never collects

Your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, files, messages, precise location, the advertising ID or the Android ID. The only permission the game asks Android for is vibration.

Who gets your data

Only Google, in the roles above. For crash reports and game events, Google processes the data for us and only we can see the reports. For ads, Google uses the data under its own policies. We do not sell, rent or give your data to anyone else.

Why we may use it (EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland)

  • Crash reports: our legitimate interest in a game that works.
  • Game events: your answer to Google's consent form. If you decline, our legitimate interest in counting, without any ID, how far players get.
  • Ads: your choices in Google's consent form.
  • Purchases: to carry out the purchase you asked for.
  • Email: to answer you.

Security

Everything the game sends is encrypted on the way. The reports are kept by Google and can be opened only through the game's own Google account.

Your choices and rights

  • Ad choices and Show ad offers, in Settings > Privacy, as described above.
  • Uninstalling the game deletes everything it kept on your phone, and its random IDs with it. A new installation gets new IDs.
  • Asking us. Nothing we receive names you, so we usually can't tell which reports are yours. You can still ask us to show, correct or delete data about you, or object to how we use it: write to pinfoldsupport@gmail.com, and we answer within a month.
  • Complaining. If you live in the EU, the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, you can also complain to your country's data protection authority.

Children

Pinfold is made for teens and adults. It is not designed for children, and we do not knowingly collect data from children under 13. If you think a child has sent us data, write to us and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

When the game starts to collect something new, for example with sign-in to Google Play Games or online play, this page changes first and gets a new date above. Earlier versions are in the page's history.
